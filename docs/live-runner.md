# Live runner (opt-in, not run on mainnet)

Back to the [README](../README.md). The provider-rehearsal commands are in the README's [mainnet runner section](../README.md#mainnet-runner-opt-in-not-run).

```sh
make test   # all local gates (about 25 min on a laptop)
make demo   # unfunded sandbox scenario, out/evidence/demo.json, out/page/index.html and out/console/index.html (about 1 min after make test)
MANDATE_LIVE=1 MANDATE_LIVE_MANIFEST=<approved manifest> make live   # opt-in; not spending approval
```

**Signer pin.** The runner pins mainnet `v1.signer` 3.18.0 (code hash `CpGX8ywXJwvWPgZFDDCTCuPJBHF7BATT51gSfc3APERJ`); preflight refuses any other signer identity before a transaction. Gate SIGNER decodes and verifies a recorded mainnet Ed25519 `sign` response produced by that code ([fixture](../fixtures/signer/v1-signer-eddsa-sign.json)). A view cannot show that this mandate's own funded `sign` call succeeds; the run's first proposal is that test.

Run `make live` from a clean checkout at exactly the tested revision named in [evidence/index.json](../evidence/index.json), after the exact setup and runner approvals. The run records its source commit, and `node verify.mjs --mainnet` fails a run whose recorded commit, spec hash or contract hash differs from the bundle it is filed in. The verifier counts a fill as a trade only when the `execute_intents` bundle on chain carries the order the mandate logged as signed in one of the run's proposals, the mandate's Intents balances moved by exactly the signed amounts across the fill's blocks, and the order's nonce became used; a successful `execute_intents` with an empty or unrelated bundle fails.

**Retry on the same accounts.** `src/live/prepare.ts` (R1-R3) normally requires a mandate that has never had a grant or an order. For an approved retry, the prepare input's `setup.reuse` names the earlier manifest and its grant epoch; R1 then requires that grant to be revoked with no key operation in flight, no verifier key left, every order closed and no recovery in flight, and still checks storage for the full record bound, keys, allowance and deposits. The contract accepts a fresh grant after revocation, and the runner's preflight accepts a revoked grant and closed orders. `runner.rpc_url` replaces the template's RPC in the manifest. A mainnet run is filed as its own bundle (`evidence/mainnet/attempt-<n>/<revision>`) with index status `mainnet-run`, bound to the certified bundle by byte-identical `run.json` and `acceptance.json`; read it with `node verify.mjs --bundle <path> --mainnet`.

`make live` refuses a manifest that does not match the chain: network, the code hashes of the mandate, verifier, signer, oracle and both token contracts, the verifier, signer and oracle versions, the mandate configuration, keys and allowances. Token decimals are compared with the mandate's configuration, not read from `ft_metadata`; that comparison is a manual pre-approval check. It enforces fee, deposit, inventory and filler caps through a hash-chained ledger that survives restarts. It verifies each step's end state from state and receipts, and stops without any replacement order on an ambiguous outcome. Mainnet also requires `MANDATE_LIVE_CONFIRM=<manifest sha256>`.

A mainnet manifest must list `expected_owner_keys`: the owner account's complete access-key list (public key, permission and nonce) read at approval. A fresh start refuses any difference; a sandbox run without the list snapshots it at preflight. Before every runner-signed transaction and every import, the signing key's chain nonce must equal the ledger's expectation exactly (an imported transaction's nonce must be the next one), and the owner's key list must still match the snapshot. An added or removed key, a changed permission, or a nonce change on a key the ledger does not track stops the run, so an owner transaction the ledger does not record is never absorbed. Resumes check the same.

Preflight also checks each signer's signing float. Before each of its pending transactions, a signer must still hold that transaction's prepaid gas at the purchase price, the higher of the gas price and the runtime's `min_gas_purchase_price`, after the worst-case fees of its earlier steps. This covers the agent's allowance and balance and the filler's and owner's balances. The prepaid gas that is not burnt is refunded later.

**Gas a function-call key must afford.** Read from mainnet on 2026-11-10 (protocol 88, nearcore 2.14.0): `gas_price` is 1e8 yocto per gas and `runtime_config.min_gas_purchase_price` is 1e9. A transaction's cost is its conversion gas at the gas price plus its prepaid gas at the purchase price. The agent key's allowance must cover that cost in full and is reduced by it. When the receipts finish, the unused gas and the difference between the purchase price and the gas price on the burnt gas return to the allowance. For the 150 TGas the runner attaches to `propose_order`:

| | NEAR |
| --- | --- |
| Allowance needed to send one proposal, at the runner's (150 + 5) TGas | 0.155 |
| Net cost of one proposal once refunds have landed, at most (all prepaid gas burnt) | 0.0155 |
| Allowance each of the two mainnet templates' three agent transactions need (`float.agent_allowance`) | 0.186 |
| Allowance cap in every template and in `network.ts` | 0.25 |

So 0.25 NEAR affords the planned proposals, one at a time: the runner awaits each transaction to FINAL, refunds included, before the next. Two 150 TGas proposals cannot be in flight together on that allowance, and once the allowance falls below about 0.151 NEAR the key is refused for a 150 TGas proposal although that much remains. From a full 0.25 NEAR that is at least 7 sequential proposals if each burnt all its gas. The mandate accepts `propose_order` with no less than 125 TGas (found by execution in the sandbox case), so a key whose allowance is below about 0.126 NEAR can send no proposal the mandate accepts: that is the exhausted state, and that remainder is never spent. An allowance sized at the gas price alone (0.0155 NEAR per proposal) sends nothing; preflight refuses it before any transaction.

**Reports.** Every session of the runner (a start or a resume) that has a ledger writes one report, in two places:

| Path | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `out/live/<manifest sha256>/reports/<sequence>.json` | The session's report, preserved. `<sequence>` is `0001`, `0002`, ... in the order the sessions of that manifest ended; each file is created exclusively and never written over. |
| `out/evidence/live-<network>.json` | The latest copy: the same bytes as the report just preserved. The page, `node verify.mjs --mainnet` and the commands in this document read this path. A later run of the same kind, under any manifest, replaces it. |

The ledger (`out/live/<manifest sha256>/ledger.jsonl`) is append-only and kept per manifest as before. So a second manifest's run replaces only the latest copy; the earlier manifest's reports and ledger stay under its own sha256. A challenge phase follows the same layout under `out/challenge/`. Each report's `environment` records `manifest_sha256`, the `ledger` directory and `manifest_steps`: the manifest's step kinds, comma-separated in manifest order (element i is step i+1). A step's verification is the assertion `step-<n>-<kind>`; attempt k > 1 of a retried step (withdrawal, storage registration) is `step-<n>-<kind>-attempt-<k>`, and the latest attempt supersedes the earlier ones. The page says a run completed every manifest step only when `manifest_steps` is recorded and the latest attempt of every step in it held, with a grant, a revoke and the recovery of both assets among them, the relay rejected for the removed key, no failed preflight check and nothing unknown; otherwise it names what is missing.

`budget.owner_inventory_max` bounds what a run may place or keep under the agent's trading authority, so a fresh start refuses a mandate holding more. A manifest that only revokes, recovers and withdraws exposes nothing and is not held to it: anyone can deposit into the mandate's Intents account, and a cap there would let a stranger block the owner's exit through the runner. Preflight lists the holdings instead (`budget.owner_inventory_listed`). A read-only manifest (checks alone) keeps the cap.

There are two mainnet templates, both unapproved; every `REPLACE_ME` value must be filled in, and the runner refuses a template as is. The required proof for the first mainnet run is the scripted fixed-proposal template, [tools/src/live/manifest.example.json](../tools/src/live/manifest.example.json), described after the list. The live-agent template, [tools/src/live/manifest.live-agent.example.json](../tools/src/live/manifest.live-agent.example.json), is optional and needs its own approval and budget; it runs the whole revocation story with small amounts, with the owner's actions signed outside the runner:
1. Grant, then one deliberately forbidden proposal that the mandate itself must refuse.
2. One live agent decision, filled and settled if the agent trades.
3. A second signed order, a fixed proposal from the manifest, withheld: the filler counter-signs a complete bundle, `simulate_intents` accepts it, and the exact bundle is kept in the ledger.
4. Revoke (`revoke_external`), then relay that bundle before its deadline.
5. Recover both assets to the owner's Intents account (`recover_external`).
6. Outside every recovery claim: register the owner's token storage only where it is missing (`register_storage`, runner-signed with the owner key), then withdraw both assets to the owner's NEAR account (`withdraw_external`).

The scripted fixed-proposal template, [tools/src/live/manifest.example.json](../tools/src/live/manifest.example.json), is the required proof for the first mainnet run and stays unapproved until the owner authorizes it: the same story with a fixed proposal instead of the agent, runner-signed `revoke` and `recover`, and no owner withdrawals.

**External owner steps.** `revoke_external`, `recover_external` and `withdraw_external` are signed outside the runner, in the owner console or a wallet; `withdraw` is the runner-signed form of the withdrawal. Before the owner signs, the runner records an intent in its ledger and writes it to `out/live/<manifest sha256>/external/step-<n>-<kind>-attempt-<k>.intent.json`. The intent names the signer, the approved key (`keys.owner_external`, else `keys.owner`), the receiver, the complete action list with exact arguments, gas and deposit bounds, and the expected end state. The signer writes `{intent_sha256, tx_hash, signer_id}` to the matching `.handoff.json` file next to it. Until then the run stops as awaiting the owner's signature. `MANDATE_LIVE_HANDOFF_WAIT_SEC` makes it poll for the file first (default 0), a rerun with `MANDATE_LIVE_RESUME=1` imports the file, and `MANDATE_LIVE_HANDOFF=<file>` supplies handoffs to an existing ledger (with `MANDATE_LIVE_RESUME=1`). The runner never submits anything for the owner. With an external wallet, the owner's cancelled-signature check happens at a step that has an action to sign and no deadline: the first recovery. The owner rejects it once in the wallet. The console keeps the action pending, because a wallet's error cannot prove nothing was sent. The owner checks that no transaction appeared and the runner is still awaiting, forgets the record ("I rejected it in the wallet"), then signs. The revocation is signed without that detour, because the withheld order's deadline is running. Before the run, after setup, the wallet check is connection only: the console reads the deployed mandate (chain id, genesis, code hash, verifier, owner), the wallet connects as the owner, and the owner controls enable. Nothing is signed, and Revoke is correctly unavailable because no grant exists yet.

The import looks the hash up at final finality and checks the chain, signer, approved key, receiver, every action and argument, gas and deposit bounds, the key's nonce, the final receipt tree and the expected state and balance changes, then charges the actual fee once. An unrelated, altered or duplicate hash, or a second hash for an intent already handed off, is refused: recorded as refused, never as success, and the run stops. If a refused handoff's transaction is final on chain and the owner paid for it, its fee is recorded and charged once against the fee cap. A transaction that is not yet final records nothing.

**Withdrawals.** Each withdrawal attempt is classified from its final receipts and the token balances before and after: withdrawn, refunded (the token transfer failed and the verifier restored the Intents balance), failed, or inconsistent. A refunded or failed attempt is a finding that leaves the Intents balance intact; a resume retries it once under a new attempt, re-running that asset's storage registration first, and counts no unit twice. The other asset's withdrawal is neither blocked nor repeated.

The relay is checked twice: before the bundle is prepared, and again after signing, immediately before the broadcast. Each check requires confirmed revocation (grant revoked, no key operation in flight, signing key absent) and at least `min_remaining_sec` (10 s) left before the order's deadline by the final block's time. Unconfirmed revocation stops the run; nothing is sent. Too little remaining time skips the relay and records it as inconclusive. A relay that is sent passes only if the verifier rejects it for the removed key, before the order's deadline, with both nonces unused and balances unchanged. An expired order, missing filler funds, a different signature error or an unreadable execution time is inconclusive, never a pass; execution counts as a revocation failure.

Classified terminal outcomes, whether passed, inconclusive, failed or skipped, are recorded and the run proceeds to recovery, exiting nonzero unless every step passed. Unresolved outcomes stop the run without resubmitting anything: a lost response, an outcome that is not final, or an RPC interruption. `MANDATE_LIVE_RESUME=1` reconciles the stopped run from chain state, then continues; a resume never resubmits a transaction whose response was lost, and no step is charged or executed twice. The relay's evidence records the deadline-sensitive phases (withheld order proposed, propose confirmed by runner, bundle simulated, revoke sent, revoke confirmed by runner, relay pre-check, prepared, broadcast) with runner wall-clock times and, where read, the final block's time remaining before the deadline. "Confirmed by runner" is when the step's terminal verification was recorded; after a lost response it is the reconciling resume, never the ambiguous response.

Regrant resistance and broken-oracle or broken-MPC scenarios stay in the sandbox gates. On mainnet, recovery only shows that its receipts call no agent, oracle or MPC; it cannot show those shared services disabled. The template's RPC is `https://free.rpc.fastnear.com`, because `rpc.mainnet.near.org` is deprecated and heavily rate-limited. For lookups of older receipts, such as the verification commands above, use the archival endpoint `https://archival-rpc.mainnet.fastnear.com`.

`make demo` replays the recorded model output, so it needs no API key. To record a new model interaction, run `tools/src/agent/record.ts` with a model provider, then list the file in `fixtures/manifest.json`.

Optional live agent, tested in sandbox only with synthetic provider answers; it has not run against a real model or on mainnet, and no live provider is qualified. In the optional live-agent template the trade is proposed by a model (`provider: "near-ai"`, NEAR AI Cloud, the only live provider, key in `NEAR_AI_API_KEY`; an explicit model id, timeout and token limit are required, with one attempt and no fallback). Before deciding, the runner reads fresh oracle prices and the mandate's balances at one final block and records them. If the USD split is within 2 percentage points of 50/50 it records no action without calling the model. Otherwise it accepts only a proposal that sells the overweight asset, no more than needed to reach 50/50 at current prices plus 1% of that amount, within the grant's limits and the runner's remaining per-order and cumulative sale budgets. Those budgets are also in the agent's snapshot and prompt; a proposal above them is rejected, never clipped, while a partial rebalance within the tighter cap is valid. A decline sends nothing. Truncated or invalid output, a reply naming a different model or a timeout is rejected locally before submission: no proposal transaction is sent, and it is recorded as a failed finding. Immediately before signing, the runner reads the prices and balances again and repeats these checks, requiring each price to stay fresh for at least `min_remaining_sec` + 15 s more; a failure there is also a local rejection. If the proposal transaction is submitted and the mandate itself refuses it (its own receipt fails with the grant unchanged, or it rejects the order while pricing), that transaction can consume gas and no trade is filled; the refusal is recorded as a failed finding, and the run continues to revocation and recovery. When no order is made, the following fill and settle are recorded as skipped, never applied to an earlier order. An ambiguous outcome stops the run for reconciliation. The request and the exact response are written to the ledger before any signing. On resume, a recorded decision is reused; a request without a recorded response stops by default, and `MANDATE_LIVE_ALLOW_NEW_DECISION=1` explicitly permits a new, recorded decision, which may incur another inference charge. This 50/50 rule is the agent's objective, not a limit the contract enforces, and model quality, latency and any NEAR AI attestation are not verified here.

[tools/src/agent/rehearsal.ts](../tools/src/agent/rehearsal.ts) rehearses the provider path without any transaction: from given observations, the sale budget and the planned inventory (recorded separately from observed balances), it builds the same snapshot and prompt, makes at most one provider call and validates the reply as the runner does. Provider qualification comes before the run that creates the first grant, so before a grant exists it decides on the planned grant: the policy of the approved manifest's grant step and the planned inventory, valued at freshly observed prices. The record labels that basis as planned and not on chain. [tools/src/agent/observe.ts](../tools/src/agent/observe.ts) reads those prices, the mandate's verifier balances and its grant (none) at one final block with view calls only, after checking the RPC's chain id and genesis against the manifest. A rehearsal qualifies a provider only when an approved real call from the requested model produced a final decision the runner would act on: an accepted proposal whose pre-signing checks pass, or a decline. A schema-valid answer that the objective, the sale budget or the pre-signing checks reject does not qualify. The record verifier re-derives the claim from the record's own fields; the provider class, the approval reference and the reported model are recorded by the operator, not proven by the record. The state file is bound to the manifest it was observed for, and the record carries that manifest's sha256. It has run with stub providers only; a real provider call is paid inference and refuses to run without the owner's explicit approval.

The planned deposit is the setup record's, in base units: for the live-agent template, about 60% USDC, for example 5 USDC + 0.91 wNEAR at the 2026-11-07 reading, `5000000` and `910000000000000000000000`, with the wNEAR units recomputed on setup day. The rehearsal refuses to run without it, and refuses a deposit above the manifest's `owner_inventory_max`. That maximum, 5 USDC + 1.25 wNEAR, is a ceiling, not the deposit: near 50/50 it can fall inside the no-call band (51.9% USDC at the 2026-11-06 prices), where the model would not be called.

## Open challenge (optional, not run)

Not run, and not part of the planned mainnet run. Running it needs a separate owner approval after a mainnet run; the flag is not spending approval.

- **Templates.** [tools/src/live/manifest.open-challenge.example.json](../tools/src/live/manifest.open-challenge.example.json) opens the challenge with a small new deposit and a fresh grant; [tools/src/live/manifest.open-challenge-close.example.json](../tools/src/live/manifest.open-challenge-close.example.json) closes it after judging (revoke, recover and withdraw). Both are unapproved.
- **Command.** `MANDATE_LIVE=1 MANDATE_LIVE_MANIFEST=<approved challenge manifest> make challenge`, against a separately approved manifest. Its ledger and evidence stay apart from the completed run's (`out/challenge/`). `make live` refuses a challenge manifest (`agent_key_published`) before any network action, and `make challenge` refuses any other manifest.
- **Files.** `out/challenge/evidence/challenge-<network>.json` is the challenge record. The runner's own evidence is one file per phase, `live-<network>-challenge-open.json` and `live-<network>-challenge-close.json`; the close does not write over the open's, and neither can write over the completed run's `out/evidence/live-<network>.json`. These are latest copies; every session's report is also preserved under `out/challenge/live/<manifest sha256>/reports/` (see Reports above).
- **Record status and earlier records.** `status` is `open` only while the challenge grant is active on chain, `closed` when the close manifest completed and the grant is revoked, `inactive` when the challenge was opened but its grant is not active and no completed close is recorded (a close in progress, for example), and `not-opened` when no opening of the grant now on chain is known. An opening counts when this run's grant transaction succeeded, its terminal verification passed and it is the grant on chain. A record already on disk is carried forward (its opening, the open manifest's hash, the publication link) only when its chain id, genesis hash, mandate account, mandate code hash and agent key equal the manifest's and the grant it opened is the grant on chain (epoch, path and, where both are known, public key). A completed close stays recorded: when that carried-forward record is `closed` and the same grant is still revoked, a refresh with the open manifest or a close re-run that does not complete keeps its `closed` status, close manifest hash and close receipts. Otherwise nothing is taken from it: the new record lists the reasons in `previous_record_ignored`, the runner's message repeats them, and the old file is kept as `out/challenge/evidence/replaced/challenge-<network>-<n>.json`. None of this runs before or between the owner's steps: the record is built from reads after the runner has returned.
- **Order: open, then publish the key, then record the link.**
  1. `make challenge` with the open manifest. The record shows the grant active and `agent_key.publication_url` is `null`.
  2. The lead publishes the agent's private key outside the repository.
  3. The same command again with `MANDATE_LIVE_RESUME=1 MANDATE_CHALLENGE_KEY_URL=<https link to the published key>`. The resume sends no transaction and rewrites the record with `agent_key.publication_url` set. The variable must be an https URL; anything else is refused before any network action. A later run without it keeps the recorded link.

  A page or README may say the key is published only when `agent_key.publication_url` is set.
- **Preflight for a published key.** The agent account must hold exactly one key: the manifest's, function-call, for the mandate, with `propose_order` as its only method and an allowance within the cap. That key must not be registered on the verifier as a signing key of the owner, filler or mandate account (`keys.published_agent_key_not_on_verifier`). To open, the key's remaining allowance and the agent account's free NEAR must each buy one proposal's prepaid gas, 0.155 NEAR at mainnet's purchase price (`float.published_agent_allowance_one_proposal`, `float.published_agent_balance_one_proposal`). The close needs none of that allowance.
- **Sandbox case.** LIVE.open-challenge runs both templates after a completed run on the exact deployment binary, with an adversary holding the agent key ([tools/src/live/challenge.ts](../tools/src/live/challenge.ts)).
- **Key.** The agent's private key is never committed to any repository or evidence bundle. Only if the owner approves running the challenge does the lead publish it outside the repository (the X thread and a pinned repository issue); only its public key is recorded here.

What it shows (`CHALLENGE_SHOWS`):

> Whoever holds the agent key cannot withdraw, transfer to another account, change keys or policy, or exceed the per-order and outstanding caps, the concession bound, the turnover cap and the loss breaker; the earlier grant's withheld order stays rejected while the challenge grant is live.

What it does not show (`CHALLENGE_DOES_NOT_SHOW`):

- It does not show that the deposit is safe from poor trades. Whoever holds the agent key can make permitted trades at up to the concession bound on each order and up to the grant's turnover cap.
- Whoever holds the agent key can exhaust the agent's gas allowance and the grant's proposal budget; the challenge then accepts no further proposals.
- The cost is bounded by the challenge deposit and the agent's remaining gas allowance.
- An unknown contract defect could lose the deposit.

Exposure, from the open template's proposed policy (turnover cap 100%, concession bound 0.5%, loss breaker 2%, oracle age 60 s, proposal charge 10%) and its 0.25 NEAR allowance cap:

- Trading at the limits costs, by design, at most the turnover cap times the concession bound: 100% x 0.5% = 0.5% of the deposit's value at the grant's initial reference. On a deposit at the template's ceiling of 5 USDC + 1.25 wNEAR (about $10 at NEAR $4) that is about $0.05.
- The loss breaker is not a loss cap. It stops new authorizations once a pricing observes a 2% drawdown from the grant's initial reference value; orders already signed stay executable until they expire or the key is removed.
- Losses beyond the concession are possible. Each order is priced against a reference up to 60 s old, so whoever holds the key can trade against a stale reference after the market has moved; market moves and verifier fees add to that (SPEC.md, Policy).
- Gas: key holders spend the agent account's NEAR up to the key's remaining allowance, at most 0.25 NEAR.
- An unknown contract defect could lose the whole deposit.

On mainnet the earlier run's withheld order would expire 60 to 120 s after it was signed, long before a challenge opens. There, "stays rejected" would rest on the earlier grant's key being absent from the verifier (`earlier_grants[].registered` is false in the challenge record), not on a fresh rejection attributable to revocation. Only the sandbox case, whose primary grant uses an 1800 s order lifetime and oracle age for this purpose, relays the unexpired withheld order while the challenge grant is live.
